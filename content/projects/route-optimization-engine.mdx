## Overview

Builds optimal daily schedules and driving routes for a field services company. Takes jobs, technician availability, and material orders; outputs assignments, sequences, and time windows in under 60 seconds. Re-plans on the fly when a job runs long or a technician calls out.

## The Problem

Dispatchers assign jobs by hand each morning and guess at travel time. One overrun, cancellation, or sick day cascades through the rest of the route. The cost shows up in fuel, missed jobs, and missed appointment windows.

Most competitors either pay for expensive off-the-shelf software that doesn't fit their workflow, or rely entirely on dispatcher intuition. A custom system trained on the company's own historical data outperforms both over time.

## Why Now

- **Free, open-source optimization libraries** (Google OR-Tools, PuLP) — the same math UPS and Amazon use, accessible without building from scratch
- **Google Maps API** — real-time traffic and routing data at low cost
- **Modern Python tooling** — fast iteration on data pipelines
- **The company already owns the key data asset** — material orders that can be converted into job-duration estimates

## Approach

Three independently-replaceable layers:

- **Ingestion** — CSV or Excel upload now; direct work-order-system API later. Inputs validated before processing; missing fields flagged immediately.
- **Optimization engine** — solves job-to-technician assignment, route sequencing, and time-window compliance simultaneously. 50–200 jobs in under 60 seconds. The objective is configurable (minimize total drive time by default; can switch to maximizing jobs completed, prioritizing high-value jobs, or balancing workload).
- **Output** — dispatcher dashboard with map and overrides, mobile-friendly technician view with arrival times and job notes, management dashboard with summary metrics, and alerts when a job runs long enough to put downstream jobs at risk.

## Core Innovation — Material-to-Time Estimation

No dispatcher guesses. Job duration comes from a predictive model trained on **materials ordered**. Each parts kit maps to a baseline; the model self-corrects as actual completion times come in. After 60–90 days of operation, predictions stabilize — including weather-driven adjustments like rainy-day slowdowns and per-technician speed differences ("Tech A is 15% faster on installs").

## Daily Data Inputs

**Jobs:** customer address, job type, materials ordered, customer availability window, priority level, special requirements.

**Technicians:** ID, starting location (home or depot), shift start/end, skills and certifications, availability and time off, historical job speed.

**Material pickup:** location(s), hours of operation, average wait time by time of day, whether materials are pre-pulled or pulled at pickup.

**External data:** Google Maps API for travel times; weather API for buffer adjustments.

## Phased Delivery

Each phase ships a working product. The company doesn't wait until phase 5 to see value.

1. **Core routing engine** — addresses in, optimized driving routes per technician out (2–4 weeks)
2. **Material-to-time estimation model** — realistic per-job time buffers from materials ordered (2–3 weeks)
3. **Skill & availability constraints** — only qualified, available technicians get assigned (1–2 weeks)
4. **Real-time rerouting** — recalculate remaining routes when a job is cancelled or runs long (2–3 weeks)
5. **Dashboards, mobile view, reporting & API integrations** — production polish (2–3 weeks)
