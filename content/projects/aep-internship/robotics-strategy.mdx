## The question

Robotics vendors were pitching AEP across power generation, transmission, and supply chain. Leadership needed a defensible way to decide where to invest, grounded in operational fit and economics rather than whichever demo looked best.

## The framework

I scored 48 candidate use cases on a four-part rubric, each on a 1 to 5 scale:

- **Impact:** safety, labor, reliability, and data value
- **Effort:** how hard it is to implement
- **Maturity:** how ready the technology is today
- **Fit:** how well it matches the company's operations

## What rose to the top

Six use cases made Tier 1. They shared a pattern: they take people out of dangerous work, like confined spaces and heights, while capturing better inspection data than manual rounds. Examples include wall-climbing inspection crawlers for boiler tubes, quadruped robots for routine plant rounds, and drones built for confined-space inspection.

## From scores to decisions

For the priorities, I built business cases that compared owning robots outright, renting them as a service, and a hybrid of the two. I also outlined a multi-year roadmap and a model for how pilots would graduate into regular operations.

I presented the findings to VP and SVP-level leadership, and the work earned buy-in for a multi-year robotics initiative.

## What I took away

A scoring rubric is only as useful as the conversation it starts. Putting all 48 options on one page made the tradeoffs visible, and it let the discussion move from "is this cool" to "is this worth it here."
